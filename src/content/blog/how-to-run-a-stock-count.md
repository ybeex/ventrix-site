The month-end count has a reputation. It's long, it's tedious, and it often ends with numbers that still don't match and nobody sure why. That's not because counting is hard. It's usually because the count was designed as an event rather than a habit.

Here is a method that makes counts shorter, calmer and more useful.

## Why counts go wrong

- **Too big.** Counting everything at once means tired people, rushed decisions and nobody left to investigate differences.
- **Not blind.** If the counter can see the expected number, they tend to confirm it.
- **Movement during the count.** Stock leaves while you count, and the numbers drift.
- **No reasons.** A difference of -4 gets "adjusted" and forgotten, so the cause (and the leak) stays.

## Step 1: Count by shelf, on a cycle

Divide your store into shelves or zones, and give each its own cycle. Fast-moving or high-value areas might be counted every 2 weeks; slow, low-value shelves every quarter. A shelf takes 15 to 45 minutes. Each week you count a few, and over a month everything gets covered.

This also tells you where problems are. If one shelf is always off, you look at that shelf.

## Step 2: Prepare

- Tell people. A short announcement ("Shelves A and B will be counted after lunch") prevents most interruptions.
- Return outstanding items first, or note them as out. Tools that are checked out are not "missing".
- Pause movement on the shelf for the count window, or record anything that moves.
- Have the right person: someone who knows the shelf, plus someone to double-check on big differences.

## Step 3: Count blind

The counter enters what they physically find for each item, without leading with the expected number. Count in the same order each time, and count in small groups (by 5 or 10) for large quantities.

For assets, confirm each one by tag or name. For consumables, count the units.

## Step 4: Compare and investigate

When the counted number differs from the system's number, do not adjust yet. First:

1. **Recount** the item, ideally by someone else.
2. **Check recent movement.** Was something used or returned and not logged?
3. **Look nearby.** Wrong shelf is the most common cause.
4. **Check open loans.** Is it checked out to someone?

## Step 5: Record a reason for every difference

This is the step that makes the process valuable. For every item that remains different after you've investigated, record *why*:

- Used and not logged
- Damaged or expired
- Miscounted previously
- Unknown (this is a real answer, and a useful one)

Over time the reasons form a pattern. If "used and not logged" dominates one shelf, you have a process problem. If "unknown" keeps showing up for high-value items, you have a security problem.

## Step 6: Approve adjustments

Changing the record is a decision, not a chore. For small differences, a Ventrix can adjust. For large or valuable ones, ask a supervisor to approve. This protects the person doing the count as much as the business. Photo evidence of the shelf is a useful addition for high-value items.

## Step 7: Close the count and act

Close the count so the record is updated and the date of the last count is noted. Then do one thing with what you learned: reorder, fix a shelf, tighten a loose process. A count that changes nothing was a waste of time.

## A simple weekly rhythm

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| Monday | Announce this week's shelves |
| Tuesday–Thursday | Count one shelf per day |
| Friday | Review differences and reasons, reorder |

Four small counts beat one dreaded one.
